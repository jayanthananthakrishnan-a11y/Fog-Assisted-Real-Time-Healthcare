Architecture A: Cloud-Only Monitoring Baseline

Direct IoT-to-Cloud Telemetry — Every single heartbeat crosses the 100 ms WAN.

1. What Happens in Plain English

Everyday Analogy & Flow
📮 Everyday Analogy: The Distant Postal Mailbox
Imagine every patient heartbeat or breath is written on a postcard and sent via postal mail to a central government office 500 miles away. If a patient suddenly enters cardiac arrest, the alarm cannot sound until the postcard arrives in the distant city, gets opened by a clerk, and a return telegram is sent back. By the time the reply arrives (~208 ms later), critical response time has elapsed.
Step 1
Sensors Capture Vitals
4 sensors per patient sample Heart Rate, SpO2, Temperature, and Blood Pressure every 1,000 ms.
Step 2
Immediate WAN Dispatch
Without any local edge filtering or inspection, all 4,000 raw readings are pushed directly across the 100 ms WAN link.
Step 3
Cloud Datacenter Processing
The remote cloud cluster processes the readings (44,800 MIPS) and determines whether an emergency alert is warranted.
Step 4
Return Downlink to Actuator
The alert traverses the 100 ms WAN downlink back to the hospital bedside monitor (Total Round-Trip: 207.95 ms).

2. How We Generated & Processed the Healthcare Data

Workload Specifications

The simulation workload generates realistic medical vital signs using calibrated clinical bounds:

  • Heart Rate (ECG): Normal 60–100 bpm, Abnormal 100–130 bpm, Critical > 130 bpm (Ventricular Tachycardia).
  • Pulse Oximetry (SpO2): Normal 95–100%, Abnormal 90–94%, Critical < 90% (Acute Hypoxemia).
  • Body Temperature: Normal 36.5–37.5 °C, Abnormal 37.6–38.5 °C, Critical > 38.5 °C (High Fever).
  • Blood Pressure: Normal 110–130 mmHg, Abnormal 130–150 mmHg, Critical > 160 mmHg (Hypertensive Crisis).
  • Distribution: 85% Normal readings, 10% Abnormal readings, 5% Critical emergencies (Random Seed 42L).

Architecture C: Naive Filtering & Aggregation

Bandwidth Saved at the Expense of Human Life — The Aggregation Window Trap.

1. What Happens in Plain English

Everyday Analogy & Flow
📦 Everyday Analogy: The 5-Letter Shipping Box Trap
To save postage costs, the hospital manager orders that no mail leaves the building until a shipping box fills up with exactly 5 letters. If a doctor writes a critical red alert saying "Patient is suffocating!", the clerk puts the emergency letter into the box and waits until 4 more routine routine letters arrive over the next 4 seconds. By the time the box fills and ships, the patient has suffered irreversible brain damage.
Step 1
Noise Filtering
Normal readings with delta < threshold are dropped (saving 14.8% traffic).
Step 2
Window Buffering (W = 5)
Readings enter a sliding FIFO buffer. They MUST wait until 5 readings accumulate before emitting.
Step 3
Emergency Trapped in Queue
Critical alerts have NO priority bypass. They sit trapped in the buffer waiting for normal vitals to fill slot 5!
Step 4
Lethal Delay & SLA Failure
Average emergency response takes 2,293.52 ms (violating the 500 ms SLA by 358%).

2. The Math of the Aggregation Window Hazard

Queueing Formula
Buffering Delay Formula:
T_buffer_wait = (W - k) × T_sampling_interval
For W = 5 and an alarm arriving at position k = 2: T_wait = (5 - 2) × 1,000 ms = 3,000 ms delay!

Critical SLA Compliance:
SLA Compliance = (Alarms < 500 ms) / Total Alarms = 63 / 226 = 27.88% (72.12% Failure Rate!)

Architecture E: Multi-Ward Two-Gateway Failover

1. What Happens in Plain English

Everyday Analogy & Flow
🤝 Everyday Analogy: The Neighboring Ward Sister
ICU Ward A and ICU Ward B are connected by a short corridor door (a 1.0 ms high-speed fiber link). If the server in Ward A suddenly crashes or reboots for a software upgrade, Ward A doesn't abandon its patients or revert to waiting for postal mail from 500 miles away. Instead, Ward A's sensors immediately hand the telemetry across the corridor door to Gateway B, which continues ringing the bedside emergency alarms in under 8 ms!
Normal State
Dual Ward Balance
Gateway A serves Ward A (Patients 1–5); Gateway B serves Ward B (Patients 6–10). Both run priority engines.
Load Balancing
When Gateway A CPU exceeds 80%, normal batching tasks are offloaded across the 1.0 ms LAN to Gateway B's spare capacity.
Outage Injected

Gateway A experiences a 20-second simulated hardware reboot. Telemetry automatically pivots to Gateway B.
Seamless Failover
100% SLA Maintained
Emergency alerts during the outage are processed by Gateway B in 8.13 ms (retaining 100% SLA compliance).
